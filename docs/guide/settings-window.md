# Settings window

**Settings** in the tray's menu opens a window where you change every setting below without editing the file. It is a small program of its own, `auto-clicker-settings`, which the daemon starts and which exits when you close it, so the daemon stays as light as before while you play. Choosing Settings again while the window is open does not open a second one; Wayland does not let the program bring the open window forward, so click it in the task manager.

- **Clicker** sets what a run does: the action, the interval (milliseconds between actions, or actions a second), jitter, the button, the key, where to click and how often. The key is picked by pressing it: choose **Change…** and press the key, or Esc to cancel. When Plasma delivers the hotkeys, the program reads no keyboard, so there is no **Change…**: type the key's name, such as `space`, or its code after `#`, such as `#113`. The fixed point is picked on the screen: choose **Pick on the screen…**, and every screen dims under an overlay; click where the clicker should click, or press Esc to cancel (the overlay also closes by itself after 30 seconds). The window fills in the point, and **Save** keeps it. Picking needs the screens from KWin and works only while nothing is clicking, recording or playing. A picked point is clicked where you clicked to within 1/65536 of the width or height of all your screens together, and always on the screen you picked it on, which on a screen's last pixel can cost another 64th of a pixel: under 0.11 logical px across a laptop panel and a 3440 px monitor side by side. It is saved with the screens it was picked on, so clicking at it starts only on those screens and stops if they change: after you dock or undock, pick it again. The point can also be typed as two numbers, which keeps no screens.
- **Hotkeys** shows the three hotkeys. To change one, choose **Change…** and press the new combination, such as Ctrl+Alt+F9, or Esc to cancel; the program's hotkeys do nothing while it waits, for up to 10 seconds. A hotkey without Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Meta fires on a plain key press, in games too, so the window asks before saving one. When the hotkeys come from Plasma's shortcuts (`hotkeys = "portal"`, or `"auto"` that picked them at start), Plasma owns the hotkeys: the window shows the ones Plasma reports, and **Change in System Settings** opens Plasma's shortcut settings for the program. This tab also sets where hotkeys come from and where clicks go (**Picked at start** is `auto`), which apply after the program restarts.
- **Playback** sets the speed and how many times a recording plays, which apply from the next playback once saved. Below them is the recordings list, newest first, with a ✓ on the one the playback hotkey plays; it shows the newest 200 and says how many older ones there are. Pick a recording to **Rename** it (the name inside its file changes, the file name stays; two recordings may share a name, and the time tells them apart), **Play** it after the tray countdown, make it the one the hotkey plays (**Play with the hotkey**), or **Delete…** it for good after a Yes/No. These apply at once, without Save, and like Save they are greyed out while something is clicking, recording or playing: stop it first. A rename that would make the file larger than a recording may be is refused, and when a change is made but may not survive a power loss, the window says so. The tray's Play menu follows every change.

**Save** checks the settings as the program checks the file, says what is wrong if something is, and writes the file. **Save** is greyed out while something is clicking, recording or playing: stop it first. Saved hotkeys work at once; the other settings apply to the next start. If the file changed since the program read it, or could not be used, the window asks before replacing it. The window speaks Swedish when the system language is Swedish, and so do the tray's menu, title and tooltip, with every notice and problem; text quoted from another program, such as an error from the system, stays as that program wrote it. The window follows Plasma's light or dark colours.
